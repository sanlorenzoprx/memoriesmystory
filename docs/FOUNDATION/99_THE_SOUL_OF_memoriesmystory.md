# The Soul of Memories: My Story

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Locked constitutional foundation

We preserve people.

Not photographs.

Not recordings.

Not transcripts.

People.

This company begins with a simple human truth: a person is more than a name, a date, or a photograph.

A person is the way they tell a familiar story. The pause before they remember a name. The laugh that changes the meaning of a sentence. The uncertainty they admit. The version of an event that only they carried. The love, work, fear, humor, sacrifice, and hope held inside an ordinary life.

When that voice disappears, a family loses more than information. It loses presence.

Memories: My Story exists to protect that presence.

## The individual memory

Every Living Family Archive begins with one person and one remembered moment.

The memory does not need to be historically important. It can be a kitchen table, a first car, a school uniform, a hurricane, a wedding, a hard year, a neighborhood, or someone just outside the frame.

The platform's first duty is to honor the memory as the storyteller gives it: in their voice, with their uncertainty, emotion, language, and point of view intact.

## The family living legacy

One Memory Story preserves a moment. Many stories begin to preserve a person. Stories connected across people and generations begin to preserve a family.

That is the Living Family Archive: not a museum that freezes the past, but a living place where relatives can listen, contribute, disagree honestly, discover relationships, and add new memories over time.

The archive lives because each generation can receive it, understand it, and continue it.

## The soul of the company

We do not exist to make AI the author of family history.

We do not exist to collect media that families cannot carry with them.

We do not exist to turn grief, age, or fear into pressure.

We exist to help one human being say, “This is what I remember,” and help another human being—today or decades from now—press Play and hear them.

Our work is quiet. If we do it well, the technology disappears and the family remains.

## The test

When priorities compete, return to the person holding the photograph.

Are we helping them remember?

Are we preserving what they actually said?

Will their family be able to receive it?

Will it still belong to them?

Will it remain understandable after our current technology is gone?

If not, we have wandered.

Ask one final question:

**Does this preserve the soul of Memories: My Story?**

If the answer is no, the implementation changes.

The soul does not.

## Our enduring reminder

We are building software.

But more importantly, we are preserving families.
