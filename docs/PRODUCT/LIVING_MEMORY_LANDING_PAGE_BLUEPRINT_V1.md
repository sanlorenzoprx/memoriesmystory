**Status:** current functional landing-page blueprint  
**Date:** 2026-08-12  
**Purpose:** single section-by-section source for the public landing page build  
**Governing sources:** Living Memory Foundation, Offer V1, Public Copy Standard V1, Proof Ledger V1, Landing Build Contract V2, Visual Direction V3  
**Commercial framework input:** supplied Hormozi-style offer/landing-page skill system

---

## 1. What this blueprint is for

This document turns the product doctrine, offer, proof rules, audience decisions, visual experiments, and conversion lessons into one page flow that design and engineering can build from.

The page is allowed to be long.

It is not allowed to feel crowded, rushed, technical, or hard to read.

> **Long is acceptable. Dense is not.**

The page must persuade by helping the visitor understand one idea at a time.

Primary conversion:

> **Create one complete Living Memory free.**

Paid conversion happens only after the visitor understands and believes the Living Memory idea.

---

## 2. Audience and readability

Initial commercial audience hypothesis:

- primary purchaser or initiator: Baby Boomer women;
- secondary purchaser or initiator: their adult daughters;
- the experience must still work for other family members and ages.

Public copy target:

- normal reading level: **grade 6–7**;
- normal maximum: **grade 8**;
- use a necessary product term only when ordinary language would lose meaning;
- explain the term the first time it appears;
- one main idea per sentence;
- short paragraphs;
- familiar words first;
- do not shorten a sentence so much that the reader loses the reason, meaning, or emotional context.

Words to avoid in normal landing copy unless explained:

- mechanism;
- provenance;
- source-grounded;
- affect;
- retrieval;
- ontology;
- canonical;
- derivative artifact;
- attributed recollection;
- bounded Share Artifact.

Translate them into normal language:

- “how it works”;
- “where the story came from”;
- “based on the real photo and voice”;
- “feeling in the voice”;
- “find it again”;
- “the original”;
- “a copy you choose to share.”

---

## 3. The page's persuasion job

The supplied landing-page framework uses:

**Hero → Problem → Outcome → Solution → Mechanism → Value Stack → Proof → Objections → Guarantee → CTA → FAQ**

MemoriesMyStory expands that into smaller, calmer sections so the visitor never has to process too much at once.

Our page flow is:

**Idea → See It → Try It Free → Problem → Stakes → Desired Future → Proof → How It Works → Ease → Muse Trust → Try It Free → Memory Circle → Privacy → Sharing → Value Growth → What You Receive → Difference → Memorial Belief → Try It Free → Paid Offer → Value Stack → Objections → Trust / Guarantee → FAQ → Final Free CTA**

Every major section should answer one question already forming in the visitor's mind.

## Section 7 — Proof of the idea: make belief reasonable

### Visitor question

Does this idea make sense, or is it just emotional marketing?

### Headline

# **Why a Living Memory makes sense.**

### Proof layer A — direct demonstration

Show again in a simple split:

**Photo alone** → **Photo + real voice + story details**

The visitor can see the difference without a testimonial.

### Proof layer B — research-supported premises

Use large, readable blocks.

#### Block 1

**Pictures can help bring personal memories back.**

Plain copy: Research on personal memory has found that pictures and visual cues can affect what people remember and how they remember it.

#### Block 2

**A voice carries more than words.**

Plain copy: A person's voice can carry identity and feeling that a written transcript cannot fully preserve.

#### Block 3

**Families often remember by talking together.**

Plain copy: People may remember different parts of the same event. Talking together can bring out details one person would not remember alone.

### Integrity rule

Research supports the idea. It does not prove that MemoriesMyStory improves memory, family relationships, health, or well-being.

### Future proof space

Reserve space for real, permissioned customer examples later:

- first Living Memory reactions;
- parent/daughter examples;
- Memory Circle examples;
- customer quotations;
- real completion and purchase evidence.

Do not imitate testimonial design before testimonials exist.

## Section 13 — Memory Circle: remember together anywhere

Sit together at the kitchen table—or join from across the country. Put one photograph in the middle and share the moment of telling its story.

### Explain the experience

One person may remember the place. Someone else remembers the year. A sister recognizes an uncle. A daughter asks the question no one thought to ask. Someone laughs before the story is even finished.

Memory Circle is designed to preserve more than a cleaned-up summary. With everyone's consent, it can preserve the moment the family remembered together.

### What can be kept

- each person's voice;
- questions and answers;
- another person's version of the memory;
- names, places, and dates someone else remembers;
- corrections and uncertainty;
- natural reactions, pauses, and laughter when captured with consent.

### Ways to join

- in the same room;
- live from another home or country;
- a mix of in-person and remote family;
- later, by adding another memory when someone could not attend.

**Live Memory Circle is included in Life and Family.**

# COPY BLUEPRINT

## 28. Copy rule: context before cleverness

Every sentence should answer at least one of these:

- What is this?
- Why should I care?
- How does it work?
- Why should I believe it?
- Is it easy?
- Is it safe/private?
- Can my family join?
- What do I get?
- What does it cost?
- What should I do next?

If a sentence sounds elegant but makes the visitor ask “what does that mean?” rewrite it.

## 29. Reading-level target

Use grade 6–7 as the normal writing target.

Grade 8 is acceptable when a necessary idea cannot be expressed naturally at a lower level.

Do **not** force grade 5 if the result sounds childish, vague, or loses emotional meaning.

### Better

“Families often remember by talking together.”

### Worse for public copy

“Collaborative remembering combines partially independent autobiographical perspectives.”

### Better

“The original photo and voice stay the source.”

### Worse

“Source-grounded canonical artifacts maintain provenance.”

# VISUAL / PACING BLUEPRINT

## 30. Rendering 3 density target

Rendering 3 should show roughly **40–50% less information in any one viewport than Rendering 2**.

This does not mean removing the persuasion content.

It means spreading it across more sections with larger type, larger images, and more space.

### Desktop targets

- body: about 20 px;
- H1: about 60–72 px where space permits;
- H2: about 40–52 px;
- line-height: about 1.55–1.7;
- major section vertical padding: about 120–160 px when appropriate;
- copy column: generally 45–70 characters per line;
- buttons: at least 48 px high, preferably larger for primary actions.

### Mobile targets

- body: 18–19 px;
- H1: about 40–48 px;
- H2: about 32–38 px;
- one column;
- generous gaps between concepts;
- no horizontal scrolling;
- no tiny icon grids;
- no hover-only information;
- pricing stacks vertically.

# PROOF BLUEPRINT

## 33. Evidence ladder

Use proof in this order as it becomes available:

1. **Direct mechanism demonstration** — visitor sees and hears Photo → Living Memory.
2. **Premise research** — supports why photos, voice, and remembering together are reasonable inputs.
3. **Technical product proof** — only for implemented/tested behavior that matters to trust.
4. **Real customer proof** — permissioned examples, quotes, completions, purchases.
5. **Commercial proof** — conversion and retention evidence once enough volume exists.

Never jump from premise research to a customer outcome claim.

# ACCEPTANCE BLUEPRINT

## 36. Landing page passes only when all are true

### Understanding

- A visitor can explain a Living Memory in normal words after the first few sections.
- The page does not depend on understanding AI terminology.
- Public copy normally reads around grade 6–7 and does not exceed grade 8 without a justified term.

### Emotional pacing

- Each major section has one dominant job.
- Rendering/code shows about 40–50% less visible information per viewport than Rendering 2.
- The page can be long without feeling repetitive or rushed.

### Readability

- Baby Boomer readers can comfortably read body copy without zooming on normal devices.
- Adult daughters still see a contemporary, premium product rather than a “senior” interface.
- Buttons and controls are obvious and large enough.

### Belief

- The Magic Moment is demonstrated before pricing.
- Proof is visible, honest, and substantial.
- Research is clearly labeled as support for the idea, not proof of customer outcomes.

### Conversion

- The free Living Memory is impossible to miss but never feels forced.
- Free invitations appear after meaningful belief/ease/trust milestones.
- Paid pricing appears late.

### Product truth

- AI is helper, not author.
- Original photo and voice remain primary.
- Privacy begins private and sharing is chosen.
- Memory Circle feels like shared remembering around a photograph, not video conferencing.
- No unimplemented feature is presented as available for purchase without fulfillment evidence.

### Dignity

- Memorial framing is moving but not morbid.
- No fake scarcity, testimonials, counts, guarantees, or urgency.
- The page respects family memory, grief, faith, and personal control.
