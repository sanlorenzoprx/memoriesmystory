**Status:** current landing-page implementation contract  
**Date:** 2026-08-11

**Product source:** Foundation + Living Memory product contracts

## 1. Why V2 exists

V1 proved the information architecture. The first two visual explorations showed a different problem: the page can contain the right information and still ask the visitor to process too much too quickly.

V2 therefore optimizes for **emotional pacing, readability, belief, and repeated low-friction access to the free Magic Moment**.

The target is not a short page. The target is a page that never feels rushed.

> **Long is acceptable. Dense is not.**

Initial audience hypothesis for design/testing: Baby Boomer women and their adult daughters are likely primary purchasers or initiators. This is a commercial hypothesis to test, not a Foundation invariant. The page must therefore be especially comfortable for older readers while still feeling contemporary and premium to adult children.

## 2. Governing conversion logic

The page must build desire in this order:

**Idea → Demonstration → Problem → Proof → Free Magic Moment → Mechanism → Ease → Muse trust → Free reminder → Memory Circle → Privacy/share → Legacy progression → Memorial belief → Free reminder → Paid offer → Risk reversal → Final free CTA**

The paid offer is not the first conversion.

The primary conversion is:

> **Create one complete Living Memory free.**

The free Living Memory is the product demonstration, the first win, and the fastest path to belief.

## 3. Hormozi-source rules adopted from the supplied offer system

The supplied landing-page skill explicitly requires:

- clarity over cleverness;
- outcome first;
- friction reduction early;
- value before price;
- proof when trust is low or claims are strong;
- logic or demonstration when customer testimonials do not yet exist;
- short sections with one clear idea;
- objection handling throughout rather than only at the end;
- multiple CTAs.

Memories: My Story adapts those mechanics to a calm family-trust brand. We repeat the opportunity to experience the mechanism; we do not repeat pressure, fake urgency, or aggressive sales devices.

## 4. Readability and pacing contract

### One main thought per section

Every major section should answer one primary question:

- Why should I care?
- What is a Living Memory?
- Why is this believable?
- Can I try it?
- Is it easy?
- What does AI do?
- Can my family join?
- Is it private?
- What can this become?
- What does it cost to preserve more?

Do not combine three persuasion jobs into one viewport merely to shorten the page.

### Desktop rhythm

Target guidance, subject to responsive testing:

- major section vertical padding: roughly 96–144 px;
- body text: generally 18–20 px with generous line-height;
- explanatory text width: usually 45–70 characters per line;
- H1: large editorial scale, approximately 56–72 px depending on viewport;
- H2: approximately 38–52 px;
- CTAs: readable label size with minimum 48 px interactive height;
- no more than 3–4 compact benefit items visible beside a major emotional visual.

### Mobile rhythm

At approximately 390 px width:

- body text should remain approximately 18 px unless accessibility testing indicates larger;
- avoid multi-column information grids;
- sections should stack into one obvious reading path;
- buttons should be large and thumb-friendly;
- pricing cards stack vertically;
- proof cards become readable story blocks, not tiny icon tiles;
- generous blank space between ideas is required.

## 6. Section 1 — Hero: one idea

Headline:

**A photograph preserves the moment. Give it its voice.**

Supporting copy:

> Turn a family photograph into a Living Memory—preserving the original photograph together with the person's real voice, story, and context.

Primary CTA: **Create Your First Living Memory**  
Secondary CTA: **See a Living Memory**

Visual: one large believable family photograph. Do not try to explain the entire product in the hero.

## 7. Section 2 — Magic Moment demonstration

This section should be allowed to breathe and should feel almost like experiencing the product.

Sequence:

1. photograph alone;
2. visitor activates **Hear the story**;
3. authentic voice/waveform begins;
4. short transcript appears;
5. restrained context such as person/place/year appears;
6. the object is named **Living Memory**.

Core copy:

> **The photograph shows what the moment looked like. The voice tells you what it meant.**

Do not put research, pricing, Memory Circle, or feature grids inside this section.

Immediately follow with CTA 2, the dedicated **Try it with one photograph** free-offer section.

## 8. Section 3 — Missing-story problem

Headline:

**Every family has photographs whose stories live in only one or two people's memories.**

Use one large photograph and only 3–4 questions at a time:

- Who are they?
- Where was this?
- What happened next?
- Why did this day matter?

Copy should establish that the problem is not file storage. It is the separation of a photograph from the human meaning behind it.

## 9. Section 4 — Proof of the premise

This is a major persuasion section, not a small credibility strip.

Headline:

**Why a Living Memory makes sense.**

Subhead:

> Before we have enough permissioned customer stories to show here, we will separate what research supports from what only our customers can prove.

### Proof layer A — Demonstration

Re-state what the visitor already experienced:

**Photo alone → Photo + authentic voice + context.**

This is direct mechanism demonstration, not a testimonial.

### Proof layer B — Research-supported premises

Use three spacious proof blocks with plain-language wording and visible source attribution:

#### Visual cues and autobiographical remembering

Approved public wording:

> Research on autobiographical memory has found that visual imagery and pictorial cues can influence how people retrieve personal memories. This supports using photographs as prompts; it does not prove that Memories: My Story improves memory.

#### A voice carries more than transcript text

Approved public wording:

> Voice-perception research describes human voices as carrying speech, identity, and affective information. That is why Memories: My Story treats the original voice as more than text waiting to be transcribed.

#### Remembering is often social

Approved public wording:

> Research on collaborative remembering and everyday reminiscence shows that people often build remembered narratives through conversation and partially different perspectives. That premise informs Memory Circle.

Each block links to the approved proof ledger.

### Proof layer C — Product proof as it becomes real

Reserved visual space for future:

- permissioned customer Living Memory examples;
- short before/after demonstrations;
- customer quotations;
- completion counts only after auditable evidence exists.

Never style premise research as a customer testimonial.

Immediately follow proof with CTA 3.

## 10. Section 5 — Mechanism

Headline:

**Turn a photograph into a Living Memory.**

Use a large, simple sequence:

**Photo → Voice → Muse → Living Memory**

The full underlying system may include context, preservation, family contribution, provenance, and retrieval, but the public visual should not force the visitor to learn internal ontology.

## 11. Section 6 — Ease

Headline:

**Four simple steps.**

1. **Pick a photograph.**
2. **Tell the story naturally.**
3. **Muse helps when useful.**
4. **Hear your Living Memory.**

Avoid dense feature descriptions. Make the low effort visible.

## 12. Section 7 — Muse and AI trust

Headline:

**You remember. Muse helps.**

Keep the first visible list to four points:

- transcribes what you say;
- helps identify missing context;
- asks thoughtful follow-up questions;
- organizes the story around the original source.

Trust statement:

> **Muse never invents your memory. The original photograph and voice remain the source.**

Follow with CTA 4.

## 13. Section 8 — Memory Circle

Headline:

**Some stories are better remembered together.**

Copy:

> Sit together at the kitchen table—or join from across the country. Put one photograph in the middle and share the moment of telling its story.

Memory Circle must visually communicate people gathered around the memory, not a generic business video call.

Show the benefits with generous spacing:

- hear the story together;
- add another person's recollection;
- identify a person, place, or date someone else remembers;
- preserve questions, reactions, corrections, and differing recollections with attribution;
- capture the telling moment itself when consented, including the natural pauses, laughter, and emotion that give the conversation meaning.

Modes:

- same room;
- remote live;
- hybrid;
- later asynchronous contribution.

Offer note:

**Live Memory Circle is included in Life and Family.**

## 14. Section 9 — Privacy and sharing

Headline:

**Private first. Shared when you choose.**

One simple visual:

**Private → Family → Friend → Facebook / chosen destination**

Copy should make clear that sharing one bounded copy does not expose the private Family Archive.

## 15. Section 10 — Moment → Chapter → Life → Family

Give this progression an entire spacious section.

- **Moment:** one photograph, one story, one Living Memory.
- **Chapter:** a meaningful part of someone's life.
- **Life:** the stories that made a person who they were.
- **Family:** a connected archive across people and generations.

Do not show pricing in this section.

## 16. Section 11 — Memorial category belief

This section should feel editorial, not like a comparison widget.

Headline:

**We've always found ways to remember the people we love.**

Copy:

> Photographs can preserve faces. Traditional memorials can preserve names and dates. A Living Memory can preserve more of the life between the dates—the voice, the story, the relationships, and the way a family remembers together.

Key line should receive generous negative space:

> **Preserve more of the life between the dates.**

Do not disparage graves, monuments, funerals, faith, cremation, or mourning practices.

Follow with CTA 5 before paid pricing.

## 17. Section 12 — Paid offer reveal

Only after the mechanism, proof, ease, trust, family value, and legacy frame have been established.

### Chapter — $247 one-time

**Preserve a meaningful chapter.**

Show no more than 5 primary benefits before an expandable **See everything included** control.

### Life — $747 one-time

**Preserve the stories that made a life.**

Include **Memory Circle** prominently.

Use wording such as **Designed for one person's fuller life story** rather than fake “most popular” evidence.

### Family — $1,497 one-time

**Build your family's Living Memory Archive.**

Emphasize multiple people, relationships, continuity, richer collaboration, and Memory Circle.

Pricing is a commercial test variable, not a Foundation invariant.

## 18. Section 13 — Objection stack

Do not create a giant wall of objections. Use 4–6 large, expandable questions initially:

- I am not good with technology.
- I do not know what to say.
- I do not know exact dates.
- I do not want AI changing the story.
- I want to keep this private.
- My family remembers it differently.

Map each answer to an existing product contract.

## 19. Section 14 — Risk reversal and trust

Do not publish a refund term until operations can fulfill it.

Permanent product trust statement:

> **Your original photograph and authentic human voice are never silently replaced by an AI-generated version.**

Future customer guarantee language requires approved Stripe/support/legal behavior.

## 20. Section 15 — FAQ

FAQ exists for remaining decision friction, not to repeat the entire page.

Prioritize:

- Is the first Living Memory really free?
- Do I need a credit card?
- How much voice can I record?
- Does AI write my memories?
- Can I keep everything private?
- Can family members contribute?
- What is Memory Circle?
- Can people join from anywhere?
- Can I export my memories?

## 22. Proof-to-customer transition

Before real customer proof exists, the page may use:

1. product demonstration;
2. technical acceptance evidence where relevant;
3. properly attributed premise research.

As real permissioned customer evidence becomes available, progressively add:

1. actual Living Memory examples;
2. customer quotations;
3. daughter/parent family stories;
4. Memory Circle examples;
5. measurable product outcomes that can be audited.

Borrowed premise proof should become less prominent as direct product proof grows.

## 23. Analytics additions

In addition to the existing funnel events, every free CTA emits:

`create_first_living_memory_clicked`

with placement metadata:

- `hero`;
- `post_demo_free_offer`;
- `post_proof`;
- `post_muse`;
- `pre_pricing`;
- `final`.

Track:

- CTA impression;
- CTA click;
- first Living Memory started;
- first Living Memory completed;
- paid offer viewed after completion;
- Chapter/Life/Family selection.

The first commercial learning question is not “Which button gets the most clicks?” It is:

> **Which belief state and CTA placement produces completed first Living Memories, and which completed first Living Memories lead to paid preservation?**

- [ ] body copy is comfortably readable for older adults;
- [ ] the Magic Moment has its own visual breathing room;
- [ ] a dedicated **Try it with one photograph** free-offer section follows the demo;
- [ ] free creation appears contextually at least five times without feeling repetitive or coercive;
- [ ] proof is a full section, not a small strip;
- [ ] proof clearly distinguishes demonstration, premise research, and future customer proof;
- [ ] Memory Circle gets enough visual space to communicate shared remembering from anywhere;
- [ ] memorial positioning is dignified and spacious;
- [ ] paid pricing appears only after the free-first path is reinforced;

- [ ] AI remains supportive rather than visually dominant;
