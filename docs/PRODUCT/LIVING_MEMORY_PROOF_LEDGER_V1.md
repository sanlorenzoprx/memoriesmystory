# Living Memory Proof Ledger V1

**Status:** approved evidence framework for pre-customer landing-page proof  
**Date:** 2026-08-11

## Purpose

Memories: My Story does not yet have enough permissioned customer evidence to make the landing page depend on testimonials, case studies, family counts, or customer outcome statistics.

Until direct product proof exists, the page may use three clearly separated forms of evidence:

1. **mechanism demonstration** — show a photograph becoming a Living Memory;
2. **premise research** — cite research that supports specific ideas behind the mechanism;
3. **technical product evidence** — show only behavior that has actually been implemented and acceptance-tested.

Research must never be presented as proof that Memories: My Story itself improves memory, well-being, family relationships, or preservation outcomes unless a study actually tested the product.

## Evidence class A — mechanism demonstration

### Claim

A photograph alone can be experienced differently from the same photograph presented together with authentic voice and selected context.

### Allowed demonstration

Show:

**Photograph → authentic source voice → transcript/context → Living Memory**

This is direct demonstration of the product concept, not scientific proof and not a customer testimonial.

### Public framing

> **See what changes when the story stays with the photograph.**

## Evidence class B — visual cues and autobiographical remembering

### Approved source 1

Williams JMG, Healy H, Ellis NC. *The effect of imageability and predictability of cues in autobiographical memory.* Memory. 1999. PMID: 10504900.  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10504900/

### What the source supports

The study reports that visual imageability of cues was associated with greater specificity in autobiographical memory retrieval.

### Approved public wording

> **Visual imagery can influence autobiographical remembering.** Research on autobiographical memory has found that the imageability of cues can affect how specifically people retrieve personal memories.

### What it does not support

Do not claim:

- every photograph improves recall;
- photographs always outperform verbal cues;
- Memories: My Story improves cognitive function;
- Memories: My Story prevents memory loss.

### Optional supporting source

El Haj M, et al. *The picture of the past: Pictures to cue autobiographical memory in Alzheimer's disease.* 2020. PMID: 33003989.  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33003989/

This study found higher autobiographical memory performance with verbal-and-visual cueing than with no cue in its studied groups. Because the study includes Alzheimer's disease, public use requires careful wording and must not imply treatment or prevention.

## Evidence class C — voice carries identity and affective information

### Approved source

Belin P, Bestelmeyer PEG, Latinus M, Watson R. *Understanding voice perception.* Br J Psychol. 2011. PMID: 21988380.  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21988380/

### What the source supports

The review describes three major categories of information processed from voices: speech, identity, and affect.

### Approved public wording

> **A voice carries more than the transcript.** Voice-perception research describes human voices as carrying speech, identity, and affective information.

### Product implication

The original human voice remains canonical and is not reduced to text after transcription.

### What it does not support

Do not claim:

- voice recordings preserve a complete personality;
- emotion can always be inferred accurately;
- AI can reliably reconstruct a person from voice;
- the transcript has no value.

## Evidence class D — collaborative remembering

### Approved source 1

Norrick NR. *Collaborative Remembering in Conversational Narration.* Top Cogn Sci. 2019. PMID: 30194752.  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30194752/

### What the source supports

The article describes collaborative remembering in natural conversation, including participants contributing partially separate perspectives to create a mosaic-like narrative.

### Approved public wording

> **People often remember together.** Research on conversational remembering describes people combining partially different perspectives and contributions while telling a shared story.

### Product implication

This supports the premise behind Memory Circle: one person's recollection can be enriched by attributed contributions from others without pretending all memories are identical.

### Approved source 2 — older-adult everyday reminiscence

Demiray B, Mischler M, Martin M. *Reminiscence in Everyday Conversations: A Naturalistic Observation Study of Older Adults.* J Gerontol B Psychol Sci Soc Sci. 2019. PMID: 29190392.  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29190392/

### What the source supports

The naturalistic study observed healthy older adults reminiscing in everyday conversations with partners, friends, children/relatives, and others, with identity, teaching/informing, and conversational functions represented.

### Approved public wording

> **Reminiscing already happens in ordinary conversation.** Naturalistic research with older adults has documented reminiscence during everyday conversations with friends, partners, and family.

### What these sources do not support

Do not claim:

- Memory Circle improves mental health;
- collaborative remembering always produces more accurate memories;
- disagreement should be resolved into one answer;
- family conversation guarantees stronger relationships.

## Evidence class E — current technical product proof

Only claim runtime behavior that has current acceptance evidence.

Current validated examples from the development line include:

- photo-first local capture;
- offline photo-to-voice continuation;
- original voice preservation/retrieval/recovery;
- original media durability contracts;
- account binding/recovery contracts;
- Living Memory domain contract;
- Cloudflare deployment dry-run success;
- phone-first Playwright coverage for the existing first experience.

Do not turn development acceptance evidence into customer outcome claims.

## Future direct product proof slots

As soon as permissioned users exist, collect evidence in this order:

1. **Living Memory before/after demo** — photo alone versus preserved photo + voice + context;
2. **first Magic Moment quotation** — what the user felt after hearing the result;
3. **daughter/parent use case** — permissioned intergenerational story;
4. **Memory Circle example** — multiple attributed recollections around one photograph;
5. **sharing loop evidence** — a recipient creates their own Living Memory;
6. **paid preservation evidence** — customer chooses Chapter, Life, or Family after completing the free Moment.

Every testimonial or example must record:

- permission status;
- source identity/attribution rules;
- media-use scope;
- whether wording is verbatim or edited;
- date collected;
- product version experienced.

## Public proof layout contract

Do not compress proof into three tiny icon cards.

Recommended landing-page treatment:

1. large headline: **Why a Living Memory makes sense**;
2. one large demonstration panel;
3. three spacious research-premise blocks;
4. visible source links/footnotes;
5. a clearly labeled future area for **Stories from families using Memories: My Story** once real evidence exists;
6. contextual free CTA immediately after proof.

## Integrity rule

> **Borrow evidence for the premise. Never borrow a customer's voice.**

Until Memories: My Story has its own proof, say exactly what the evidence supports and no more.
