# Living Memory Landing Page Build Contract V1

**Status:** implementation contract; page code intentionally follows this contract  
**Date:** 2026-08-11  
**Commercial source:** `LIVING_MEMORY_OFFER_V1.md`  
**Product source:** Foundation + Living Memory product contracts

## 1. Objective

Replace the obsolete product positioning with one public experience that makes a stranger understand and desire the Living Memory mechanism before asking them to evaluate pricing.

The landing page must sell in this order:

**Idea → Problem → Demonstration → Mechanism → Trust → Family/Social Value → Offer → Risk Reversal → Action**

The page is software-first. AI is integrated as invisible leverage, not presented as the product category.

Primary public conversion:

**Create Your First Living Memory — free**

The free product demonstration is one complete Living Memory and should lead to the canonical Magic Moment and `first_living_memory_completed`.

## 2. Value Equation applied

### Dream outcome

The people I love can inherit more than photographs. They can hear the authentic voice and understand the story, people, places, relationships, and meaning behind the moment.

### Increase perceived likelihood

Demonstrate the mechanism rather than merely claim it: show a photograph, play the real voice, reveal transcript/context, then present the resulting Living Memory.

Before real customer testimonials exist, use only product demonstrations, technical evidence, and properly attributed premise/research proof. Never fabricate customer proof.

### Reduce time delay

The first Magic Moment should be achievable within the first five-minute experience target.

### Reduce effort and sacrifice

The customer does two emotionally natural things:

1. choose a photograph;
2. talk about what they remember.

The software handles preservation workflow, transcription, source-grounded organization, and gentle Muse assistance behind the scenes.

Mobile-first header requirements:

## 5. Section 2 — Interactive/visual Magic Moment demonstration

Purpose: prove the mechanism before explaining features.

State A: **Photograph** — visually beautiful but contextually incomplete.

User taps **Hear the story**.

State B: authentic voice begins; waveform and captions appear.

State C: context appears gently: people, place, approximate date, relationship.

State D: label changes to **Living Memory**.

Copy:

> The photograph shows what the moment looked like. The voice tells you what it meant.

No fabricated customer quote is used.

## 6. Section 3 — Make the missing-story problem tangible

Headline:

**Every family has photographs whose stories live in only one or two people's memories.**

Around one photograph, surface unanswered questions:

- Who are they?
- Where was this?
- What were you celebrating?
- Who took the picture?
- What happened next?
- Why did this day matter?

The problem is not lack of storage. The problem is separation of image from human meaning.

## 7. Section 4 — Premise proof, not fake product proof

Three compact evidence cards may support the Idea/mechanism:

1. **Photographs can help cue autobiographical remembering.**
2. **A voice carries information beyond the literal transcript, including identity and affect.**
3. **People remember socially and may build richer accounts together from different perspectives.**

Implementation rule: every research statement requires an approved citation/source record before public publication. Do not imply a cited study tested Memories: My Story unless it actually did.

As real permissioned customer evidence becomes available, add it separately as product proof rather than silently replacing or mislabeling premise proof.

## 8. Section 5 — Name the mechanism

Headline:

**Turn a photograph into a Living Memory.**

Visual sequence:

**Photo → Voice → Muse → Context → Preserved → Family**

Supporting copy:

> Choose the photograph. Tell the story naturally. Memories: My Story preserves the authentic source while Muse quietly helps with transcription, context, organization, and the next useful question.

## 9. Section 6 — Show how little work the person does

Four steps:

1. **Pick a photograph.** Capture a physical photograph or choose a digital one.
2. **Tell the story.** Speak naturally for up to the offer's supported source duration.
3. **Muse helps when useful.** One thoughtful prompt can recover missing context without turning the experience into an interrogation.
4. **Hear the Living Memory.** The photograph, original voice, transcript, and confirmed context come back together.

CTA repeat: **Create Your First Living Memory**.

## 10. Section 7 — Explain AI only after the value is clear

Headline:

**AI works behind the memory, not instead of it.**

Copy:

> Muse can help transcribe, organize, retrieve, connect, and gently prompt. The photograph and authentic human voice remain the source. Muse never gets to silently decide what someone remembered.

Show a simple source hierarchy visual:

**Original photo + voice → human corrections/contributions → structured context → AI-derived presentation**

## 11. Section 8 — Memory Circle: share the moment of telling the story

Headline:

**Some stories become richer when the people who remember them are together.**

Core copy:

> Memory Circle lets family and friends gather around one photograph and share the moment of telling its story—whether they are sitting on the same couch, joining from different cities or countries, or adding a recollection later.

The visual must not resemble generic videoconferencing marketing.

Preferred composition:

- selected photograph/Living Memory is the central visual anchor;
- active storyteller is prominent but secondary to the memory;
- other participants appear around the photo;
- visible but quiet record/consent state;
- optional Muse prompt is restrained;
- attributed recollections can be seen accumulating without obscuring the conversation.

Supporting copy:

> One person remembers the place. Another remembers the year. Someone recognizes an uncle. Someone laughs before the story is even finished. Memories: My Story can preserve not only the final facts, but the source moment your family remembered together.

Offer label:

**Live Memory Circle is included in Life and Family.**

Link: **See how Memory Circle works**.

## 12. Section 9 — Privacy and voluntary sharing

Headline:

**Private first. Shared when you choose.**

Copy:

> Every Living Memory begins under your control. Keep it private, invite family, send it to a friend, or deliberately share a selected copy to places such as Facebook. Your private archive does not become public because you shared one memory.

Show a simple choice model:

**Private → Family → Friend → Facebook / chosen destination**

Do not use share-to-unlock, referral credits, or coercive copy.

## 13. Section 10 — Value ladder

Headline:

**Start with one moment. Build the story of a life.**

Visual progression:

**Moment → Chapter → Life → Family**

Definitions:

- **Moment:** one Living Memory;
- **Chapter:** a meaningful collection around a period, place, relationship, or theme;
- **Life:** Chapters and Living Memories across one person's life;
- **Family:** an archive connected across people, relationships, and generations.

## 14. Section 11 — Memorial category belief

Headline:

**We've always found ways to remember the people we love.**

Supporting copy:

> Photographs preserve faces. Traditional memorials preserve names and dates. A Living Memory can preserve more of the life between the dates—the voice, the story, the relationships, and the way the family remembers together.

Comparison presentation should be restrained, not combative.

| Traditional memorial can preserve | Living Memory can preserve |
| --- | --- |
| name | authentic voice |
| dates | stories from the life between them |
| inscription | the person's own words |
| photograph | the context behind photographs |
| static record | attributable family enrichment over time |

Prohibited framing:

- “better than a grave”;
- “don't waste money on a funeral/monument”;
- fear countdowns around death;
- disrespect toward faith, burial, cremation, monuments, or mourning traditions.

## 16. Section 13 — Value/objection stack

Organize benefits by customer concern, not software subsystem:

- **“I don't know what to say.”** → Muse prompts.
- **“I don't know the exact date.”** → uncertainty remains honest.
- **“I don't want to type.”** → authentic voice + transcription.
- **“I have too many photos.”** → Chapters + source-grounded organization.
- **“My family remembers more than I do.”** → contributions + Memory Circle.
- **“We remember it differently.”** → attributed alternate recollections.
- **“I want it private.”** → private first.
- **“I want to share this one.”** → bounded voluntary sharing.
- **“I don't want AI changing the story.”** → originals remain canonical.
- **“I started on my phone.”** → cross-device continuity target.

Avoid fake line-item dollar values for internal software functions.

## 21. Mobile wireframe contract

At ~390 px width the first screen should contain, in order:

1. compact brand/navigation;
2. Idea eyebrow;
3. H1;
4. one short supporting paragraph;
5. primary CTA;
6. secondary demo CTA;
7. concise privacy/trust line;
8. Living Memory hero visual beginning above or close to the fold.

Avoid an oversized navigation bar, a wall of text, tiny controls, horizontal scrolling, or requiring the user to understand pricing before the mechanism.

Each later section should be one clear thought per viewport-scale block. Pricing cards stack vertically on mobile with the offer outcome before the feature list.

## 22. Visual design system

Target feeling:

**Apple Photos warmth + Airbnb trust + Calm pacing + editorial family archive + conversion clarity.**

Use:

- generous negative space;
- warm off-white/paper surfaces;
- restrained dark ink typography;
- emotionally believable photography;
- subtle archival texture rather than faux-antique decoration;
- large readable type;
- soft depth around source photographs;
- waveform/audio UI that feels human rather than technical;
- quiet chips for people/place/date context;
- accessible contrast and controls.

Do not use:

- generic AI gradients as the primary visual language;
- robots/brains/sparkle overload;
- fake countdown timers;
- red urgency banners;
- giant crossed-out fake valuations;
- stock photos of call centers or generic SaaS dashboards;
- gravestones as fear imagery in the hero.

## 25. Claims ledger

Every externally verifiable claim must be categorized before publication:

- **Product proof:** implemented and acceptance-tested behavior.
- **Premise proof:** external research supporting the Idea/mechanism, correctly cited.
- **Commercial hypothesis:** price, package, conversion expectation, or planned offer.
- **Planned capability:** designed but not yet fulfillment-ready.
- **Prohibited unsupported claim:** customer counts, success rates, permanence, certification, storage duration, fake testimonials, false scarcity.

The page must never render a commercial hypothesis or planned capability as an already-proven product fact.

## 26. Acceptance tests for the eventual React implementation

The landing implementation is not complete until automated and manual evidence shows:

1. No mail-in digitization, Memory Kit, box-shipping, technician-cleaning, or unrelated physical-service positioning remains on the primary page.
2. The H1 teaches the photograph/story problem without leading with AI.
3. Primary CTA creates/continues the recoverable first Living Memory path.
4. One free Living Memory is clearly communicated without share-to-unlock.
5. A visitor can understand Photo → Voice → Living Memory without reading the full page.
6. Muse is represented as assistive and source-grounded.
7. Memory Circle visibly supports shared remembrance from anywhere and preserves the telling moment rather than looking like generic video chat.
8. Privacy-first and voluntary external sharing are both visible.
9. Chapter $247, Life $747, and Family $1,497 appear only after value/mechanism sections.
10. Memory Circle is included only in Life and Family in Offer V1.
11. No unimplemented capability is represented as fulfilled without an explicit status treatment.
12. No fabricated testimonial, customer count, permanence promise, or false scarcity appears.
13. Mobile viewport has no horizontal overflow and maintains accessible tap targets and typography.
14. Keyboard navigation reaches all CTAs and interactive demo controls in a logical order.
15. Page preserves existing capture/recovery E2E behavior.
16. Typecheck, lint, unit/integration, build, Cloudflare dry run, and Playwright gates remain green.
